# Freeciv AI algorithm and efficiency audit

CivCraft revision `bc2cd83c35` and pinned Freeciv revision `3efab1385e`. The comparison covers the default AI for the Normal campaign, its shared server advisors, and the data access behind those decisions. The strongest case for adopting a Freeciv method is city danger assessment. The same city and enemy movement profile recur across guard selection, port safety, invasion planning and production advice.

## What the late-game run establishes

Seed 42, tiny map. Each figure is a mean per turn. Overall time comes from the original campaign. Function times come from its instrumented replay. The spans are inclusive and can overlap.

| Metric | Turns 76–80 | Turns 101–105 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cities | 39.0 | 44.0 |
| Units | 104.6 | 213.0 |
| Whole turn | 3.69 s | 19.49 s |
| Guard charge selection | 0.40 s | 5.90 s |
| Reachable-site search within guard selection | 0.064 s | 0.117 s |
| Port threat checks | 0.12 s | 2.43 s |
| Invasion threat checks | 0.15 s | 0.99 s |
| Rampage point-to-point route calls | 999 | 4,090 |
| Time inside those route calls | 0.17 s | 0.73 s |

City count grew 13%, unit count doubled and mean turn time grew 5.3×. The guard's own movement search accounts for only 0.12 s of the late 5.90 s. These intervals identify multiplying work and do not establish an exponential complexity bound.

A separate five-turn A/B replay started both builds from the same late save. Reusing compatible, untransported arrivals **within one city threat scan** cut uncached point-to-city searches from about 13,490 to 10,672 per turn. Time inside those searches fell from 10.30 to 7.82 s. Whole-turn time fell from 19.67 to 17.41 s (11.5%). Recorded snapshots and settlement events matched across the pair. Both arms used the saved start because a reload can diverge from uninterrupted play. This is a CivCraft experiment. A native Freeciv wall-clock comparison would require equivalent workloads. The experiment was separate from the audited commit.

## Verification for closing a gap

Start with scan-local threat reuse, then compare city-danger sharing and bounded rampage as separate changes. The late save is a short feedback loop. Compare per-turn decisions and snapshots, unreachable results, unit and city counts, routes built and elapsed time. For a deliberate decision change, compare the target, score, tie order and refresh timing against the pinned source using a focused scenario. Use a fresh seed-42 run for the 200-turn test, with interval means and the existing growth stop. Exercise aircraft, paradrop and diplomacy separately if the campaign never reaches them. The seed-42 results support prioritization through turn 105. Later-era rankings and total savings across interacting changes remain unmeasured.

## Implementation and verification

The implementation pass covers all seven numbered findings and all fourteen source comparisons. The table records the reuse boundary for each change. Coverage here refers to the identified repeated work. The broader gameplay-policy differences in the coverage section remain separate.

| Audited decisions | Change in the Rust implementation |
|---|---|
| City arrivals and danger consumers | Untransported enemies with compatible movement share positive and negative arrivals in one city scan. Guard selection retains hostile danger per city through the owner's unit-dispatch loop and recomputes garrison defense. Ground invasion shares threat predicates within its assignment pass. Port pickup checks for eligible troops before danger or routing. |
| Rampage, attack order and explorer safety | Rampage builds one bounded predecessor map per attempt, checks reachable hostile stacks and reconstructs the winning route. Enemy goals are terminal. A move or attack starts a new search. Attack order and explorer danger iterate local stacks. |
| Tile, owner and cargo membership | Unit planning indexes tile arrivals, owner lists and city locations, and reads boarding order for cargo. Creation, movement, ownership transfer and loss update the lists. The runtime index is rebuilt on entering planning and excluded from saves. |
| Offensive support and military amortization | A target search sums assigned helper strength and cost by destination once. Nearby helpers still contribute separately. Ground mission selection shares owner and home-city production during assignment. Military build advice shares the owner's mean production in its advisor window. |
| Research prerequisites and effect valuation | An owner knowledge table shares ordered prerequisite slices and era between readers. Gains and losses invalidate both. Scoring iterates the actual prerequisites and reads slice lengths for step counts. The effect pass uses owner/class totals and refreshes them after spending. |
| Wonder and Worker production | Military research feedback takes the production pass's chosen wonder city. Eligible virtual workers share city and Worker counts by owner and continent. |
| Founder search, tile valuation and Worker displacement | Founder planning reuses city radius, spacing and ordinary yield data. The search overlays current reservations with self-exclusion and evaluates centers separately. Founding invalidates worker-phase maps. The displacement ceiling uses the acting owner's unit count. |
| Taxes, solvency and diplomacy | Tax management already forecasts each owned city once before changing rates. Solvency reuses gold support included in that forecast and refreshes expected income after a successful sale. War appraisals share the current city output contribution across player pairs. Building appraisal and obsolescence remain buyer-specific. |
| Personality, hunters, air and paradrop | Selected personality defaults resolve once. Normal and fuel path iterators yield positions incrementally. Aircraft reuse attack selection, discover static airfields once within movement and check current base state and decks. Strategic rebasing builds the winning path. Tactical drops enumerate the legal radius and preserve the existing order for ties. |
| Continents, ferries and guards | Continent IDs are shared within unit planning and assault pickup defers the first request until a port is viable. Registry validation uses indexed IDs for tracked ferries, passengers and guard targets. Full reconstruction remains available on planning entry and load. |

### Matched late-save replay

Both release executables loaded the same after-turn-100 campaign and ran five turns sequentially on the same host, with identical profiling and snapshot options. All six city, unit and lifecycle snapshots matched, including the initial and final states. Settlement event streams also matched. Both runs ended with 44 cities and 219 units.

| Metric | Baseline | Implementation |
|---|---:|---:|

The measured whole-turn reduction was 45.7%. The new rampage planner averaged 44.6 bounded searches and 480 expanded tiles per turn. The native comparison and five-turn result establish the improvement for this saved position. Individual later-era roles were exercised by the repository tests.

### Fresh seed-42 campaign

The existing growth guard stopped the 200-turn attempt at the turn-95 checkpoint. Four consecutive five-turn medians were 2.91, 3.95, 4.99 and 6.67 seconds. Each increase exceeded 15%, the last median exceeded twice the first, and an exponential fit had lower squared error than a linear fit across those four points. This is a local growth signal.
