# Civilization III: Conquests culture rules and evidence

## Target and source priority

Target: the installed Conquests campaign rules in
`../source/Conquests/conquests.biq`, with SHA-256
`1bd610ab1420217f7d64a9214b60c0bc7686e4781a0d6e66590171535495609c`.

Use sources in this order:

1. The installed Conquests BIQ for edition-specific configured values.
2. The installed Conquests Civilopedia for Conquests gameplay descriptions.
3. The supplied Civ III manual for mechanics shared with the base game; label
   it as base-game text where Conquests may differ.
4. Controlled observations from the installed Conquests game for formulas the
   official text and BIQ do not specify.

OpenCiv3 is a reverse-engineering and implementation lead, not a gameplay
authority. Its implementation must not fill gaps by itself. Freeciv/Civ2Civ3
behavior is not an expected Civ III rule.

## Extracted ruleset data

`data/rulesets/civ3_conquests/culture_biq.json` is generated by
`tools/export_civ3_conquests_culture.py`. Every exported record carries its BIQ
section/index and source hash.

| BIQ section | Extracted data | Current confidence |
|---|---|---|
| `BLDG` | All 83 building records, culture, production cost, maintenance, required building/advance, obsolete advance, Civilopedia key, and raw flags | Source bytes and record offsets validated; gameplay applicability still needs rule checks for obsolete, captured, and wartime-footing cases |
| `CULT` | Six named culture-ratio records, ratio numbers, propaganda chance, initial and continued resistance chances | Values extracted; exact selection/roll procedure not established |
| `RULE` | Town/City/Metropolis size names and caps; six culture-level names; raw parser field `BorderExpansionMultiplier=1000`; `BorderFactor=10` | Values extracted; exact field semantics and mapping to border thresholds/geometry not established |
| `RACE` | 32 civilization records and raw culture-group index | Values extracted; group index semantics should remain separate from numeric culture totals |
| `GAME` | Cultural-victory flag, one-city threshold `20,000`, all-cities threshold `100,000`, `RetainCulture` capture setting, domination terrain/population values | Values extracted; the main BIQ has `RetainCulture=0`; the installed release notes identify captured-city culture retention as an MPTournament change, whose BIQ has `RetainCulture=1` |
| `GOVT` | Assimilation chance by government; government-pair interaction fields | Assimilation values extracted; pairwise `ResistanceModifier` semantics are unverified and must not be treated as occupation-resistance rules without validation. Fascist culture suppression is stated in Civilopedia text |
| `DIFF` | All eight difficulty records, including citizen content, cost, corruption, military-law, and support settings | Exported for comparison; no explicit culture field appears, and adjacent difficulty values are not assumed to modify culture |

The BIQ stores values, not the engine formulas that consume them. Keep the
export as source data; do not directly make every field live gameplay behavior
until its interpretation is established.

previous civilization's city ledger, transferring the city total to the captor,

unconditionally preserves prior civilization entries, so that capture behavior
remains a known mismatch until the setting's accounting effect is established.

## Explicit gameplay statements

### Culture accumulation

- Conquests Civilopedia: improvements and Wonders add culture each turn; city
  culture accumulates; cited expansion examples include 10, 100, 1,000,
  10,000, and 20,000; building/Wonder contribution doubles after 1,000 years.
  Source: `../source/Conquests/Text/Civilopedia.txt`, `GCON_Culture`.
- Base manual: lists culture-per-turn values for buildings and Wonders and says
  cultural improvements produce half their normal culture during wartime
  footing. Source: `civ3_manual.pdf`, printed pages 114–115. These values are
  base-game evidence; the installed Conquests BLDG values take precedence for
  the Conquests catalog.
- Edition-specific caveat: Conquests `GCON_Mobilization` says Mobilization is
  unlocked by Nationalism and describes its production/build restrictions, but
  does not say culture output is halved. The base-manual phrase “wartime
  footing” has not been established as equivalent to Conquests Mobilization.
  CivCraft currently has no Mobilization state to consult. Therefore culture
  halving during Mobilization is unresolved for this Conquests ruleset and is
  not applied based on the base-manual wording alone. Evidence: installed
  `../source/Conquests/Text/Civilopedia.txt`, `GCON_Mobilization`; base manual,
  printed page 114.
- Conquests Civilopedia `GOVT_Fascism` explicitly says newly conquered cities
  do not generate culture until a popular majority has been assimilated. CivCraft
  now sets a serialized suppression flag when a Fascist capture leaves the
  owner's nationality at no more than half the population; accumulation resumes
  once the owner's citizens exceed half. The majority boundary is a literal
  interpretation of the explicit phrase, but equality behavior has not been
  checked in a controlled game scenario.
- The same Fascism entry says newly conquered cities lose population but gives
  no amount or sequence. CivCraft already applies its generic one-citizen
  capture loss; whether Fascism adds another loss remains unresolved and may
  affect how quickly the assimilation majority is reached.
- Base manual: civilization culture is the combined culture points accumulated
  by its cities. Source: printed page 131.

### Borders and territory

- Conquests Civilopedia ties city border expansion to city culture thresholds.
- Base manual says cultural development determines the city’s sphere of
  influence; squares inside it are territory and provide visibility and
  connected resource benefits. Source: printed pages 111–115.
- The supplied base-game manual also says a city’s cultural influence grows as
  time passes and improvements are built (printed pp. 55–57), and describes
  greater city culture as encompassing more area (p. 115). Its City Display
  reference says the culture box shows the amount needed for the next expansion
  (p. 182). These passages confirm the culture-to-border relationship, but do
  not specify the exact claimed tiles, footprint geometry, cadence, or conflict
  resolution. This is base-game manual evidence; the Conquests Civilopedia is
  the edition-specific authority where details differ.
- BIQ `RULE.BorderExpansionMultiplier` and `RULE.BorderFactor` are available in
  the export. Neither the Civilopedia nor the extracted values alone establishes
  the exact threshold sequence, expansion cadence, contested-claim tie breaks,
  or border geometry. Those remain observation targets. The member name
  `BorderExpansionMultiplier` is inherited from the OpenCiv3 BIQ layout parser;
  it is not verified Conquests editor terminology.
- OpenCiv3 investigation lead only: its city code derives expansion level from
  the base-10 logarithm of city culture, and its territory code applies tile
  rank and culture comparisons. The comments cite community forum rules; the
  Conquests BIQ border parameters are not used in those calculations. Neither
  that code nor its cited community source is authoritative evidence for this
  migration, so no part of that formula is adopted without Conquests
  verification. A controlled threshold test should compare culture immediately
  below and at 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 20,000 while holding city size and
  map context fixed; contested-claim tests should independently vary rank and
  city culture.
- OpenCiv3 save-format lead only: `../openciv3/QueryCiv3/SavSections/City.cs`
  identifies `NextCultureExpansionPower` and comments that 1, 2, and 3 map to
  thresholds 10, 100, and 1,000. This is consistent with some Civilopedia
  examples but does not account for the cited 20,000 threshold; it is not used
  to infer the remaining threshold sequence.
- Additional community leads, not accepted as Conquests rules: the GameFAQs
  Conquests strategy guide describes five city-culture bands (0–9, 10–99,
  100–999, 1,000–9,999, and 10,000–99,999) and gives corresponding territory
  footprints of 9, 21, 37, 57, and 81 tiles. A CivFanatics editor-rules post
  says `Border Factor` controls the culture needed for expansion using an
  exponential expression. These are secondary accounts, not controlled
  observations or official documentation; their scope/version and the latter
  expression's relationship to this BIQ's `BorderExpansionMultiplier` are not
  established. The guide's 10,000–99,999 band also does not explain the
  Civilopedia's explicit example of an expansion at 20,000. Use these reports
  only to design an observation matrix: measure footprint and border claims
  immediately below, at, and above each Civilopedia threshold, including
  20,000, while recording both raw `RULE` values. Also observe the civilization
  culture-level label around candidate thresholds to determine whether the raw
  `BorderExpansionMultiplier=1000` value controls those levels. Sources checked
  2026-11-10: [Denouement, Civilization III:
  Conquests Strategy Guide, “Cultural Borders”](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/915016-civilization-iii-conquests/faqs/18251);
  [CivFanatics, “Editor Rules - Explained”](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/editor-rules-explained.210035/).
- Parameter-name lead, not yet confirmed from the installed Conquests help:
  Civ III patch notes mirrored by GameFront say “Border Expansion Multiplier”
  was renamed “Lvl. Multiplier,” while a CivFanatics editor reference describes
  Level Multiplier as setting civilization culture-level thresholds and Border
  Factor as affecting city border-growth thresholds. This suggests the raw
  `RULE.BorderExpansionMultiplier=1000` field may configure civilization culture
  levels rather than the city-border formula. The evidence is a patch-note
  mirror and community explanation, not the Conquests editor's page body; retain
  the raw field and value, but do not use this interpretation as an implemented
  rule until the installed help or controlled Conquests observations confirm
  it. Sources checked 2026-11-10: [Civ III v1.29 patch notes mirror](https://www.gamefront.com/games/civilization-3/file/civilization-iii-v1-29-patch);
  [CivFanatics, “Editor Rules - Explained”](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/editor-rules-explained.210035/).

### Captured cities, resistance, nationality, and assimilation

- Conquests Civilopedia says relative civilization culture determines whether
  foreign nationals resist after capture; resisters do not work. It says ending
  the war or garrisoning stronger forces can quell resistance.
- Base manual adds that resisting citizens cannot work, occupation forces end
  resistance over time, and culture, government, and luxuries affect how fast;
  nationality can persist after resistance and later assimilation. It also says
  a city with any resisters cannot rush production. Sources: printed pages
  104–105, 116–117, and 131–133.
- Edition-specific wording differs on how resistance ends: the base manual says
  troops are the only way to quell it (printed p. 116), while the Conquests
  Civilopedia explicitly also allows ending the war. For this Conquests target,
  the Civilopedia statement governs; the peace-resolution path is implemented.
  The manual's recapture exception (previously ruled citizens do not resist,
  p. 117) remains unconfirmed for Conquests and is not implemented.
- The BIQ `CULT` section contains initial and continued resistance chances plus
  culture-ratio values; `GOVT` contains assimilation chances. Their turn-by-turn
  selection, rolls, and interaction with garrison strength, peace, government,
  and luxuries have not yet been established.
- The base manual says recaptured citizens may not resist if previously ruled
  by the current owner, while newly created citizens may. This needs a
  controlled Conquests scenario before treating it as an expansion rule.

### Civilization-level effects and victories

- Conquests Civilopedia explicitly distinguishes five global culture groups
  (Amerindian, Greco-Roman, European, Far Eastern, Middle Eastern) from culture
  points. It says groups determine city/citizen appearance, same-group nations
  tend to start near each other, and they are more tolerant in diplomacy.
  Source: `../source/Conquests/Text/Civilopedia.txt`, `GCON_Culture`,
  “Global Cultures.” The behavior is explicit qualitatively, but the BIQ's
  `RACE.CultureGroup` plus `GAME.CulturallyLinkedStart` do not specify the
  placement algorithm or diplomacy adjustment. The main BIQ's linked-start
  flag is false, so no culture-group start clustering is inferred for this
  campaign. The diplomacy adjustment remains unimplemented pending an
  official formula. The renderer's city-style mapping is separate and must
  not be treated as the gameplay culture-group rule.
- Base manual says relative civilization culture affects resistance,
  diplomacy, city defection, and cultural victory; it describes cultural
  defection as a nearby city switching sides under a sufficiently large culture
  imbalance. Sources: printed pages 131–132 and 153–154.
- Conquests Civilopedia says civilization culture is the sum of city culture
  values (“the sum of the cultural values of all of your cities”) and that
  larger cultural advantage is favorable in resistance, diplomacy, and
  competing territorial claims. CivCraft now sums the culture ledger entries
  only from cities currently owned by that civilization. The per-civilization
  city ledger remains intact on capture so a later recapture can restore that
  civilization's city culture; whether `GAME.RetainCulture` changes this
  storage behavior remains unresolved.
- Conquests Civilopedia gives a 20,000-point single-city cultural victory and
  lists civilization totals of 60,000 / 80,000 / 100,000 / 130,000 / 160,000
  for Tiny through Huge maps. The installed BIQ `GAME` fields specify 20,000
  and 100,000, matching the one-city threshold and Standard-map total.
- The threshold values are explicit, but culture attribution for a captured
  city is not. The one-city check uses the current owner's ledger entry, an
  inference from “one of your cities.” Whether a captured city's retained
  value under `GAME.RetainCulture=1` is credited to the new owner or original
  civilization remains unresolved pending observation.
- The installed `DIFF` records contain difficulty parameters but no explicit
  culture modifier. Their `MilitaryLaw` value is `1` at all eight levels; it is
  not applied to culture or occupation resistance without supporting evidence.
- Conquests Civilopedia lists cultural victory among its six basic win paths.
  The BIQ uses default victory conditions; enabling this path in CivCraft's
  default campaigns follows that official description. Custom victory toggles
  are not represented in the current campaign setup.
- Exact formulas for diplomacy, city defection, and culture comparison bands
  remain unverified. Custom map sizes have no Civilopedia civilization-total
  threshold and are left without culture-victory evaluation.

## Current CivCraft gap inventory

- `src/sim/score.rs::record_history` still accumulates legacy building values
  into the score-only `history` value. It no longer drives the cultural
  achievements or the new culture ledger, but the old score calculation remains.
- `src/sim/borders.rs` explicitly retains city-size-based border growth as an
  approximation.
- `src/sim/cities.rs::capture_city_if_needed` transfers ownership/nationality
  and applies capture effects, but does not initialize resistance from
  culture comparisons. The city resistance count is currently test-set only.
- `GAME.RetainCulture` is exported but not consumed by capture logic.
  Per-civilization city-ledger entries persist unconditionally; whether the
  false setting should clear them or the true setting should transfer/retain
  them in another form remains unresolved.
- `src/sim/achievements.rs` now checks the new culture ledger for its
  app-specific culture achievements. Their reward score remains separate from
  Conquests culture and cannot contribute to culture totals or victory.
- `City` now has serialized `resisting_citizens` counts by nationality.
  Counted resisters are excluded from tile labor and the specialist
  count, matching the Civilopedia's explicit statement that they cannot work.
  A peace treaty now clears the signatory nationality's resisters in cities
  owned by either signatory, following the Civilopedia's documented
  war-ending resolution. Capture code does not set this count yet, and
  garrison-based resolution remains unresolved. Save/load of the counts is
  tested.
- CivCraft has no civilization Mobilization state. Base-manual half-output
  wording cannot currently be applied to Conquests without confirming that it
  matches the installed edition's Mobilization behavior.

## Implementation progress

- City state now has a serialized culture ledger by civilization and a
  completion year for culture-producing buildings. Ownership transfer retains
  prior civilization totals and creates an entry for future output from the
  new owner.
- Runtime culture rates load from `culture_buildings.tsv`, generated from the
  installed BIQ. All 46 positive-output BLDG records map to the current
  CivCraft catalog. Sistine Chapel and Knights Templar were appended as new
  catalog records at unused IDs, with their BIQ culture, cost, prerequisite,
  and obsolescence fields extracted and mapped.
  The installed records are `BLDG[38]` (Theology, culture 6, raw cost 60) and
  `BLDG[81]` (Chivalry, culture 2, obsolete at Steam Power, raw cost 30); both
  carry BIQ SHA-256 `1bd610ab1420217f7d64a9214b60c0bc7686e4781a0d6e66590171535495609c`.
- Per-city culture accumulation and 1,000-year doubling are wired into city
  processing. The Civilopedia/manual establish the doubling threshold, while
  first accrual timing on the completion turn still needs controlled
  confirmation.
- Culture output is suspended after Fascist capture until the owner's citizens
  form a majority of the population. The Civilopedia explicitly states this
  Fascism rule; strict-majority arithmetic, including equality at 50%, is an
  inferred boundary pending observation.
- These values are separate from the legacy `history` score counter. Culture
  achievements now read the new ledger; the score counter itself remains a
  legacy approximation used only in score standings.
- The resistance state implements source-explicit labor exclusion and
  resolution when the two parties make peace. Capture initialization and
  garrison-based progression are not implemented: the installed editor-help
  page bodies could not be extracted in this environment, and the original
  Conquests runtime is unavailable for controlled observations.
- Rush production is rejected when a city has any resisters, following the
  supplied base-game manual's explicit rule (printed p. 117). The Conquests
  Civilopedia does not mention rushing in its resistance entry, so this is
  recorded as base-manual evidence rather than a Conquests-specific confirmation.
- Playable civilizations now retain the raw BIQ `RACE.CultureGroup` index in
  their rules record. It remains separate from culture-point accumulation;
  group-specific gameplay effects are unresolved.
- Cultural victory now checks the current owner's single-city culture against
  20,000 and civilization culture against the Civilopedia threshold for the
  five named map sizes. The Standard 100,000 total is cross-checked against
  `GAME.AllCitiesCultureWin`. Other `GAME` flag combinations and custom-size
  scaling remain unsupported pending source settings or observation.

## Required controlled observations

Before implementing formulas not explicit above, create isolated Conquests
scenarios and record the starting save, BIQ hash, turn/year, settings, action,
and observed result for:

1. Per-building culture output, obsolete building behavior, and mobilization
   half-output.
2. Culture doubling at building age boundaries and capture behavior in paired
   scenarios differing only in `GAME.RetainCulture`: record each
   civilization's city/civilization culture and the city borders before capture,
   immediately after capture, and after several turns; repeat after recapture.
3. City expansion thresholds and timing, recording the actual footprint size
   and shape as well as ownership; test immediately below, at, and above 10,
   100, 1,000, 10,000, 20,000, and 100,000. Then
   test ownership conflicts at equal and unequal city culture/rank, including
   contested map edges. Community-reported territory footprints are hypotheses
   to compare against the game, not expected outcomes.
4. Initial/continued resistance by citizen nationality and culture-ratio band;
   changes when war ends, garrison strength changes, luxuries change, or the
   government changes; worker assignment and food/output effects while
   citizens resist.
5. Assimilation rates and cultural city defection, including government and
   nationality effects.
6. Effective one-city and civilization-wide cultural-victory settings for
   standard map sizes.

If an experiment cannot isolate a factor or the game does not expose enough
state, keep the mechanic unresolved and document what evidence is missing.

## Completion checklist

- Culture per turn and per-civilization city totals are separate from score
  history and are saved/restored.
- Culture output uses extracted Conquests BLDG data and verified age,
  obsolescence, and any edition-specific production-state rules established by
  the sources or controlled observations; unresolved Mobilization behavior is
  documented and is not guessed.
- Border expansion and contested ownership use verified culture rules and BIQ
  parameters; city size no longer acts as the expansion proxy.
- Capture, resistance, non-working citizens, resistance resolution, and
  assimilation use verified Conquests rules and retained nationality history.
- Culture-based civilization comparisons and cultural victories are wired only
  where the exact behavior is established.
- Deterministic source-backed scenarios and save/load checks cover the above;
  the project desktop gameplay scenario suite passes.
- Every unsupported rule remains explicitly listed with its evidence gap; no
  Freeciv or OpenCiv3 behavior is presented as verified Conquests behavior.
