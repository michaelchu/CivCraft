# Civilization III: Conquests culture rules and evidence

## Target and source priority

Target: the installed Conquests campaign rules in
`../source/Conquests/conquests.biq`, with SHA-256
`1bd610ab1420217f7d64a9214b60c0bc7686e4781a0d6e66590171535495609c`.

Use sources in this order:

1. The installed Conquests BIQ for edition-specific configured values.
2. The installed Conquests Civilopedia for Conquests gameplay descriptions.
3. The supplied Civ III manual for mechanics shared with the base game; label
   it as base-game text where Conquests may differ.
4. Controlled observations from the installed Conquests game for formulas the
   official text and BIQ do not specify.

OpenCiv3 is a reverse-engineering and implementation lead, not a gameplay
authority. Its implementation must not fill gaps by itself. Freeciv/Civ2Civ3
behavior is not an expected Civ III rule.

export as source data; do not directly make every field live gameplay behavior
until its interpretation is established.

The setting aligns with `../source/Conquests/readme.txt`, which specifically
says MPTournament captured cities retain culture. The main Conquests BIQ has
the setting disabled. CivCraft now clears a captured city's per-civilization
culture ledger under that default setting. This is an inference from the raw
flag and release-note contrast, supported by the CivFanatics Info Center's
reverse-engineered description that captured cities normally lose their
existing culture points ([“Culture,” captured cities](https://civfanatics.com/civ3/infocenter/),
checked 2026-11-10). A Conquests editor-rules reference says enabling
`RetainCulture` keeps the conquered city's culture instead of dropping it to
zero ([Vi.ki, “Editor and Modding”](https://civ3.vi.ki/editor-and-modding),
checked 2026-11-10). A Conquests strategy guide separately says that cities
remember the amount of culture each civilization generated there, even after
capture ([Denouement, “Cultural Borders”](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/915016-civilization-iii-conquests/faqs/18251)).
The CivFanatics Info Center likewise contains conflicting statements: its
culture section says civilization totals never decrease on capture, that a
losing civ loses cultural points when a city joins another, and that all
existing city culture points are lost on capture. These may refer to distinct
current-city, historical-per-civilization, and empire-total values; the sources
do not reconcile them. The enabled MPTournament variant is not yet wired as a
selectable runtime ruleset.

The CivFanatics Info Center also reports that captors receive no culture from
existing structures, and that an original owner who recaptures the city resumes
building culture without the old age bonus. CivCraft now records the builder
civilization for each culture-producing structure, suppresses its output while
another civilization owns the city, and restarts its age at capture. This is a
community reverse-engineering lead, not official Civilopedia text or a
controlled Conquests observation; see [“Culture,” captured cities](https://civfanatics.com/civ3/infocenter/),
checked 2026-11-10.
OpenCiv3 save-format lead only: `../openciv3/QueryCiv3/SavSections/City.cs`
parses 32 per-leader culture values in each city record and comments that
recaptured cities regain their previous culture. This supports the storage
shape used by CivCraft, but does not establish which operation
`GAME.RetainCulture` performs or when those values survive a capture.

The installed editor help, `../source/Conquests/Civ3ConquestsEdit.chm`, has
indexed topics named `Culture_Ratio.htm`, `Resistance_Chance_Culture_.htm`,
`Continued_Resistance_Culture_.htm`,
`Assimilation_Chance_Government_Properties_.htm`,
`Resistance_Selector_Government_Properties_.htm`,
`Resistance_Modifier_Government_Properties_.htm`,
`Border_Expansion_Multiplier.htm`, and
`Border_Factor_Culture_Level_Properties_.htm`. The topic names are visible in
the CHM directory strings, but the compiled page bodies have not been
extracted or read; treat this only as a lead to stronger parameter
documentation, not as evidence for field behavior.

## Explicit gameplay statements

### Culture accumulation

- Conquests Civilopedia: improvements and Wonders add culture each turn; city
  culture accumulates; cited expansion examples include 10, 100, 1,000,
  10,000, and 20,000; building/Wonder contribution doubles after 1,000 years.
  Source: `../source/Conquests/Text/Civilopedia.txt`, `GCON_Culture`.
- Base manual: lists culture-per-turn values for buildings and Wonders and says
  cultural improvements produce half their normal culture during wartime
  footing. Source: `civ3_manual.pdf`, printed pages 114–115. These values are
  base-game evidence; the installed Conquests BLDG values take precedence for
  the Conquests catalog.
- Base manual explicitly states that Wonder culture points continue to accrue
  even after an advance makes the Wonder's special ability obsolete (printed
  p. 101). The installed Conquests BIQ's `BLDG[32]` gives the Colossus 3 culture
  per turn and Flight as its obsolete advance. CivCraft does not suppress its
  culture after Flight; `obsolete_conquests_wonder_keeps_its_culture_output`
  confirms this rule path against those Conquests values. This applies the base
  manual's explicit shared mechanic to the Conquests record; it is not a
  controlled original-game observation.
- Edition-specific caveat: Conquests `GCON_Mobilization` says Mobilization is
  unlocked by Nationalism and describes its production/build restrictions, but
  does not say culture output is halved. The base-manual phrase “wartime
  footing” has not been established as equivalent to Conquests Mobilization.
  CivCraft currently has no Mobilization state to consult. Therefore culture
  halving during Mobilization is unresolved for this Conquests ruleset and is
  not applied based on the base-manual wording alone. Evidence: installed
  `../source/Conquests/Text/Civilopedia.txt`, `GCON_Mobilization`; base manual,
  printed page 114.
- Conquests Civilopedia `GOVT_Fascism` explicitly says newly conquered cities
  do not generate culture until a popular majority has been assimilated. CivCraft
  now sets a serialized suppression flag when a Fascist capture leaves the
  owner's nationality at no more than half the population; accumulation resumes
  once the owner's citizens exceed half. The majority boundary is a literal
  interpretation of the explicit phrase, but equality behavior has not been
  checked in a controlled game scenario.
- The same Fascism entry says newly conquered cities lose population but gives
  no amount or sequence. CivCraft already applies its generic one-citizen
  capture loss; whether Fascism adds another loss remains unresolved and may
  affect how quickly the assimilation majority is reached.
- Base manual: civilization culture is the combined culture points accumulated
  by its cities. Source: printed page 131.

### Borders and territory

- Conquests Civilopedia ties city border expansion to city culture thresholds.
- Base manual says cultural development determines the city’s sphere of
  influence; squares inside it are territory and provide visibility and
  connected resource benefits. Source: printed pages 111–115.
- The supplied base-game manual also says a city’s cultural influence grows as
  time passes and improvements are built (printed pp. 55–57), and describes
  greater city culture as encompassing more area (p. 115). Its City Display
  reference says the culture box shows the amount needed for the next expansion
  (p. 182). These passages confirm the culture-to-border relationship, but do
  not specify the exact claimed tiles, footprint geometry, cadence, or conflict
  resolution. This is base-game manual evidence; the Conquests Civilopedia is
  the edition-specific authority where details differ.
- BIQ `RULE.BorderExpansionMultiplier` and `RULE.BorderFactor` are available in
  the export. The Civilopedia establishes example thresholds and that they
  trigger expansions; the extracted fields alone do not establish their engine
  semantics, expansion cadence, contested-claim tie breaks, or border geometry.
  Those remain observation targets. The member name
  `BorderExpansionMultiplier` is inherited from the OpenCiv3 BIQ layout parser;
  it is not verified Conquests editor terminology.
- OpenCiv3 investigation lead only: its city code derives expansion level from
  the base-10 logarithm of city culture, and its territory code applies tile
  rank and culture comparisons. The comments cite community forum rules; the
  Conquests BIQ border parameters are not used in those calculations. Neither
  that code nor its cited community source is authoritative evidence for this
  migration, so no part of that formula is adopted without Conquests
  verification. The current implementation uses the official text's listed
  thresholds for claim-rank eligibility; controlled Conquests tests should
  still compare immediately below, at, and above each threshold while varying
  city size and map context, then independently vary claim rank and city culture.
- OpenCiv3 save-format lead only: `../openciv3/QueryCiv3/SavSections/City.cs`
  identifies `NextCultureExpansionPower` and comments that 1, 2, and 3 map to
  thresholds 10, 100, and 1,000. This is consistent with some Civilopedia
  examples but does not account for the cited 20,000 threshold; it is not used
  to infer the remaining threshold sequence.
- Additional community leads, not confirmed as Conquests rules: the GameFAQs
  Conquests strategy guide describes five city-culture bands (0–9, 10–99,
  100–999, 1,000–9,999, and 10,000–99,999) and gives corresponding territory
  footprints of 9, 21, 37, 57, and 81 tiles. A CivFanatics editor-rules post
  says `Border Factor` controls the culture needed for expansion using an
  exponential expression. These are secondary accounts, not controlled
  observations or official documentation; their scope/version and the latter
  expression's relationship to this BIQ's `BorderExpansionMultiplier` are not
  established. The guide's 10,000–99,999 band also does not explain the
  Civilopedia's explicit example of an expansion at 20,000. Use these reports
  as provisional implementation and observation leads: measure footprint and
  border claims immediately below, at, and above each Civilopedia threshold,
  including 20,000, while recording both raw `RULE` values. Also observe the civilization
  culture-level label around candidate thresholds to determine whether the raw
  `BorderExpansionMultiplier=1000` value controls those levels. Sources checked
  2026-11-10: [Denouement, Civilization III:
  Conquests Strategy Guide, “Cultural Borders”](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/915016-civilization-iii-conquests/faqs/18251);
  [CivFanatics, “Editor Rules - Explained”](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/editor-rules-explained.210035/).
- New reverse-engineering lead, not yet adopted: the CivFanatics explanation
  of editor rules says `BorderFactor=X` makes successive city-border thresholds
  `X^n`; a scenario author reports testing `BorderFactor=7` and observing 7,
  49, 343, etc. With the installed BIQ's `RULE.BorderFactor=10`, this predicts
  10, 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000. That agrees with the first four
  Civilopedia examples but conflicts with its explicit 20,000 example. Another
  CivFanatics scenario discussion also reports exponential thresholds, but is
  not official documentation. The exponential sequence is a strong
  Conquests-target lead, not a reconciled rule until the 20,000 discrepancy is
  resolved. Sources checked 2026-11-10: [“Editor Rules - Explained,” Border
  Factor](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/editor-rules-explained.210035/)
  and [“Civ1 clone in Civ3,” scenario discussion](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ1-clone-in-civ3.667077/).
- The CivFanatics Info Center independently corroborates the first four
  thresholds as 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000 and says the amount increases by a
  factor of ten each time. Its wording does not state whether that sequence
  continues unchanged after 10,000. Thus it corroborates the ordinary-game
  progression through 10,000 but does not reconcile the next threshold with
  Conquests Civilopedia's 20,000 example. Source checked 2026-11-10:
  [CivFanatics Info Center, “How can your borders expand?”](https://civfanatics.com/civ3/infocenter/).
- A CivFanatics FAQ answer lists the same four tenfold thresholds and says a
  city does not reach 100,000 because it wins a cultural victory at 20,000.
  This gives secondary support for 20,000 being the final normal-game
  expansion before a city-culture victory ends play. The FAQ presents this as
  general Civ III guidance rather than a Conquests-specific observation, so it
  does not establish what happens beyond 20,000 when cultural victory is
  disabled or in a custom BIQ. Source checked 2026-11-10: [CivFanatics,
  “Civilization III: Frequently Asked Questions,” “When do my cultural borders
  for a city expand?”](https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/).
- More detailed reverse-engineering lead: CivWiki's “Regeln der Kulturgrenzen
  (Civ3)” summarizes the CivFanatics investigation “The Eight Laws of Border
  Dynamics” (forum thread 106882). It says a city that passes its nth border
  threshold gains claims at distance and rank n+1; the thresholds are `10^n`;
  lower claim rank wins; same-rank claims compare the claiming cities' culture,
  then founding age, followed by a directional tie-break. It also says ocean
  claims are capped at rank 2 and gives two diagonal-fill rules. The author
  explicitly limits the rules to the first two cultural rings and reports
  unexplained exceptions in rings three and four. This is a player-derived
  model, not official documentation or controlled evidence from the installed
  Conquests build. It materially improves the investigation plan, but does not
  yet justify adopting the deeper-ring rules or changing the current
  civilization-culture comparison: the Civilopedia describes civilization
  culture as the factor when rival civilizations both have a legitimate claim,
  while this account's same-rank comparison uses local city culture. Its
  `10^n` sequence also conflicts with the Civilopedia's explicit 20,000
  example after 10,000. Preserve these conflicts for controlled resolution.
  Source checked 2026-11-10: [CivWiki, “Regeln der Kulturgrenzen (Civ3)”](https://civ-wiki.de/wiki/Regeln_der_Kulturgrenzen_%28Civ3%29), which attributes the rules to [CivFanatics, “The Eight Laws of Border Dynamics”](https://forums.civfanatics.com/showthread.php?t=106882); the forum page was not retrievable during this check.
- Current implementation status: `borders.rs` now removes city population as the
  border-growth input. It uses the Civilopedia's explicit city-culture thresholds
  through 20,000 to grant claim ranks (starting at rank 1 for a new city), then
  caps growth at the last threshold stated there. The FAQ gives secondary
  support for that cap during ordinary victory-enabled play; behavior beyond
  20,000 with victory disabled remains unresolved. The ring-distance, rank-first
  claim rule, and same-rank tie order are based on the CivWiki/CivFanatics
  reverse-engineering lead; applying them to the installed Conquests build is an
  inference pending controlled observation. Same-rank cross-civilization claims
  compare total civilization culture as the Civilopedia states, then local city
  culture and founding turn as the community account suggests. Its directional
  final tie-break is not implemented. The `BorderFactor=10` prediction of a
  100,000 threshold conflicts with the Civilopedia's 20,000 example; current
  code does not extrapolate beyond 20,000. This cap and the deeper-ring geometry
  remain known fidelity gaps, not verified Conquests behavior.
- Technical geometry lead, not adopted as a gameplay rule: C3X's open-source
  Conquests executable mod has an ordered cultural-neighbor table and uses a
  city's engine `cultural_level` as a ring limit for its configurable work
  area. Its source labels successive coordinate rings and reports work-area
  tile counts, but it does not implement or document the stock game's
  territory-claim selection. Its extended work-area counts differ from the
  Conquests guide's reported territory footprints, although those may be
  different measures. This is useful for building geometry tests and
  understanding the engine's level field, not for
  deciding ownership or claiming parity. Sources checked 2026-11-10: [C3X
  project explanation](https://github.com/maxpetul/C3X) and [C3X
  `injected_code.c`, cultural-neighbor table and work-ring limit](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/maxpetul/C3X/master/source/injected_code.c#L389-L527).
- Territory-claim lead from a 2004 CivFanatics player investigation: one post
  says candidate territory is constrained by city cultural rings; when multiple
  cities can claim a tile, the nearest ring level wins, then the stronger
  city's culture border, then earlier founding on an exact culture tie. It
  reports diagonal gap tiles filling in as well. Other participants disagree
  about edge cases and tile stealing, and the discussion does not establish a
  clean rule for the installed Conquests version. This yields targeted tests
  (adjacent vs. diagonal gaps, overlapping rings, unequal local city culture,
  and founding-order ties), but is not used to define stock Conquests claims.
  Source checked 2026-11-10: [CivFanatics,
  “Border calculation”](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/border-calculation.75552/).
- Territory comparison implementation is a source-based synthesis rather than
  confirmed original-game behavior: lower community-described claim rank wins;
  among equal ranks, Civilization III Civilopedia `GCON_Culture` says greater
  civilization culture favors the civilization, followed by local city culture
  and founding turn per the CivFanatics reverse-engineering account. Exact
  claim eligibility, same-turn directional ties, island/coast behavior, and
  deeper-ring geometry still need controlled Conquests observations. The
  deterministic synthetic `city_borders_playtest` checks culture thresholds,
  fog eligibility, and both directions of a same-rank civilization-culture
  comparison; it validates these implementation branches, not original
  Conquests behavior.
- Parameter-name lead, not yet confirmed from the installed Conquests help:
  Civ III patch notes mirrored by GameFront say “Border Expansion Multiplier”
  was renamed “Lvl. Multiplier,” while a CivFanatics editor reference describes
  Level Multiplier as setting civilization culture-level thresholds and Border
  Factor as affecting city border-growth thresholds. This suggests the raw
  `RULE.BorderExpansionMultiplier=1000` field may configure civilization culture
  levels rather than the city-border formula. The evidence is a patch-note
  mirror and community explanation, not the Conquests editor's page body; retain
  the raw field and value, but do not use this interpretation as an implemented
  rule until the installed help or controlled Conquests observations confirm
  it. Sources checked 2026-11-10: [Civ III v1.29 patch notes mirror](https://www.gamefront.com/games/civilization-3/file/civilization-iii-v1-29-patch);
  [CivFanatics, “Editor Rules - Explained”](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/editor-rules-explained.210035/).

### Captured cities, resistance, nationality, and assimilation

- Conquests Civilopedia says relative civilization culture determines whether
  foreign nationals resist after capture; resisters do not work. It says ending
  the war or garrisoning stronger forces can quell resistance.
- Base manual adds that resisting citizens cannot work, occupation forces end
  resistance over time, and culture, government, and luxuries affect how fast;
  nationality can persist after resistance and later assimilation. It also says
  a city with any resisters cannot rush production. Sources: printed pages
  104–105, 116–117, and 131–133.
- Edition-specific wording differs on how resistance ends: the base manual says
  troops are the only way to quell it (printed p. 116), while the Conquests
  Civilopedia explicitly also allows ending the war. For this Conquests target,
  the Civilopedia statement governs; the peace-resolution path is implemented.
  The manual's recapture exception (previously ruled citizens do not resist,
  p. 117) remains unconfirmed for Conquests and is not implemented.
- The BIQ `CULT` section contains initial and continued resistance chances plus
  culture-ratio values; `GOVT` contains assimilation chances. Initial and
  continued resistance are implemented from the reverse-engineered formula
  below; the precise Conquests turn ordering still lacks controlled evidence.
- The Conquests Civilopedia's `GCON_Assimilation` says foreign nationals
  assimilate into the majority nationality after being part of a minority
  population. It does not specify the odds or turn procedure. The installed
  BIQ stores `GOVT.AssimilationChance` values, and CivFanatics' editor-rules
  reference describes that field as the percent chance per turn for a citizen
  to assimilate. A Firaxis executive producer answered a concrete 4-versus-3
  citizen example by confirming that assimilation waits until the city
  nationality is the strict majority, then has a chance per turn to assimilate
  foreign citizens. CivCraft therefore waits until the owner's nationality is
  above half of the population, then rolls the BIQ chance independently for
  each foreign citizen and transfers successful citizens to the owner. The
  target rule is supported by Conquests text and the Firaxis explanation; the
  executive-producer post predates Conquests, and the independent per-citizen
  draw interpretation comes from secondary editor help, so both still need
  Conquests confirmation. Base-manual modifiers from relative culture and
  luxuries are not applied because no formula or Conquests-specific BIQ field
  is established. Sources checked 2026-11-10: [CivFanatics, “Editor Rules -
  Explained”](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/editor-rules-explained.210035/);
  Barry Caudill, Firaxis Executive Producer, “Beating the Culture Flip,” post
  #13 (2002-03-22), [Apolyton forum archive](https://apolyton.net/forum/miscellaneous/archives/civ3-general-archive/57183-beating-the-culture-flip).
- Assimilation evidence gap found on 2026-11-10: Conquests Civilopedia
  `GCON_Assimilation` says a foreign national assimilates after being part of a
  minority population “longer than not,” which explicitly describes a duration
  condition. In a pre-Conquests forum discussion, a community participant
  proposes that an individual must spend more time as a minority than as a
  majority (post #12); Firaxis Executive Producer Barry Caudill confirms in
  post #13 that assimilation has no chance until the right circumstances are
  present, and then uses the strict-majority 4-vs-3 example to confirm a
  per-turn chance. The exact tenure counters and their reset behavior are not
  specified by the Civilopedia or BIQ. The current implementation checks only
  whether the owner's nationality is presently a strict majority, so it
  establishes the documented gate but does not track the Civilopedia's
  duration condition. A faithful implementation needs citizen or cohort
  majority/minority tenure that survives save/load. The FAQ article describes
  resisters as a state over foreign nationals, but neither it nor the
  Civilopedia establishes whether assimilation can occur while a citizen is
  resisting; leave that interaction unresolved rather than inferring it.
  Sources: installed Conquests Civilopedia `GCON_Assimilation`; [“Beating the
  Culture Flip,” posts #12–13](https://apolyton.net/forum/miscellaneous/archives/civ3-general-archive/57183-beating-the-culture-flip).
- The installed BIQ exports eight governments and an 8-by-8 raw
  `GOVT.ResistanceModifier` matrix, including values from -10 to +5; its record
  and opposing-government indices and source hash are preserved in
  `culture_biq.json`. `DIFF.MilitaryLaw` is 1 on all eight difficulty records.
  The base-government matrix use is supported by the reverse-engineered
  source below, as is interpreting `MilitaryLaw` as the per-unit quelling cap.
  The latter use is community-sourced rather than confirmed in Conquests.
  Conquests-only government behavior remains inferred. The newer governments'
  matrix entries also show why the six-government community table cannot be
  copied directly into Conquests gameplay.
- Reverse-engineered implementation, checked 2026-11-10: CivFanatics' “The
  Inner Workings of Resistance Revealed” reports per-citizen initial
  resistance chances selected by civilization-culture ratio and adjusted by
  the captor/defender governments. The article says its formula came from tests
  and credits Firaxis developer Mike B. The six CULT base chances and their
  3x, 2x, equal, 3:4, and 1:2 thresholds in the installed BIQ match its
  reported table; its zero-culture rule says a cultureless captor is treated
  as disdainful, even at 0:0. `src/sim/resistance.rs` applies this formula
  during city capture and uses the BIQ `GOVT.ResistanceModifier` matrix as
  percentage-point adjustments. The six base-government matrix entries match
  the article's adjustments. Applying the same interpretation to Conquests'
  added Fascism and Feudalism entries is an inference, not independently
  confirmed.
  The implementation selects the first CULT ratio threshold met while
  checking the bands from highest to lowest. The article gives ratio examples
  but does not specify selection for every intermediate ratio; use of the BIQ
  values as lower bounds for those intervals remains an inference pending a
  controlled observation.
  The culture totals are snapshotted immediately before ownership transfer;
  the sources do not establish whether the original game evaluates the totals
  before or after that transfer. The linked test archive was unavailable, and
  no controlled Conquests runtime observation was made.
- Continued resistance uses the CULT continued chance and the same government
  modifier as initiation. CivFanatics says the continued base chance is 10
  percentage points below the initial chance, and that the number quelled per
  turn is capped by eligible ground-combat units times the difficulty
  “citizens quelled by military” value. Its report says unit strength does not
  matter and excludes air, sea, artillery, settlers, and workers. Conquests'

- Conquests Civilopedia explicitly distinguishes five global culture groups
  (Amerindian, Greco-Roman, European, Far Eastern, Middle Eastern) from culture
  points. It says groups determine city/citizen appearance, same-group nations
  tend to start near each other, and they are more tolerant in diplomacy.
  Source: `../source/Conquests/Text/Civilopedia.txt`, `GCON_Culture`,
  “Global Cultures.” The behavior is explicit qualitatively, but the BIQ's

  not be treated as the gameplay culture-group rule.
- Base manual says relative civilization culture affects resistance,
  diplomacy, city defection, and cultural victory; it describes cultural
  defection as a nearby city switching sides under a sufficiently large culture
  imbalance. Sources: printed pages 131–132 and 153–154.
- Conquests Civilopedia `GCON_Conversion` explicitly says low-culture cities in
  low-culture civilizations may defect and lists foreign nationals, propaganda,
  civil disorder, foreign territory, and a foreign capital as increasing risk;
  ground combat units, We Love the Day, and proximity to the city's own capital
  reduce it. `GCON_We_Love_The_King_Day` further says WLTKD halves defection
  chance and requires a non-starving city with no resisters or unhappy citizens,
  more happy citizens than content citizens, and typically size 6. These are
  explicit qualitative Conquests rules, but no probability or resolution
  procedure is given. `GAME.cultural_conversions_flag` is extracted as `false`

  `GAME.DefaultGameRules=1`. The Civilization III Complete manual says “Allow

  investigation reports default-game mode uses engine defaults instead of the
  raw scenario switches. The exporter therefore records the effective setting
  as ON, with the override interpretation labeled inferred pending controlled
  observation. The supplied base manual independently describes defection.
  Sources: `../source/Conquests/Text/Civilopedia.txt`,
  `GCON_Conversion` and `GCON_We_Love_The_King_Day`; [CivFanatics editor rules
  explanation](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/editor-rules-explained.210035/),
  [CivFanatics BIQ comparison](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ3-biq-compare.320673/page-3),
  and [Civilization III Complete manual, “Allow for Cultural Conversions”](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/steam/apps/3910/manuals/manual.pdf?tI=).
- Reverse-engineered formula candidate for city flips (secondary evidence,
  checked 2026-11-10): CivFanatics' FAQ gives
  `P = ((F + T) * Cc * H * (Cte / Cty) - G) / (2000 * D)`, where `F` is
  foreign citizens with resisters counted twice, `T` is foreign-controlled
  tiles in the city's 21-tile radius, `Cc` is 2 when the challenger has more
  local city culture and 1 otherwise, `H` is 2 for disorder / 0.5 for WLTKD /
  1 otherwise, `Cte/Cty` is total challenger-to-defender civilization culture,
  `G` is the defender's garrison count, and `D` is based on relative capital
  distance. CivFanatics' FAQ labels the tile count “working tiles under foreign
  control,” while Firaxis staff's earlier explanation says the count is the
  number of the 21 city-radius tiles inside the challenger's borders. Treat
  `T` as foreign-owned tiles in that radius; the exact count has not been
  checked against Conquests.
  Firaxis staff member Dan Magaha reported in 2002 that the relative weights
  were: foreign citizens and foreign territory as base values; capital-distance
  ratio; civilization culture ratio; historical city culture (challenger ahead
  doubles chance); disorder doubles; WLTKD halves; and land combat garrison
  subtracts. This staff explanation supports the factors and multipliers, not
  all constants in the later formula. It predates Conquests, so use as a
  candidate model, not verified Conquests behavior. Sources: [CivFanatics FAQ,
  “Culture flipping”](https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/), [Dan Magaha (Firaxis),
  “Culture-Flipping Exposed”](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/culture-flipping-exposed.14337/),
  [CivFanatics forum formula discussion](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/how-do-you-prevent-disposing-defecting.72932/),
  and [CivFanatics discussion of the distance modifier](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/d-2000-in-flip-formula.41933/).
  The first two are the strongest evidence here: the FAQ is a secondary
  synthesis; Magaha attributes the qualitative factors to a conversation with
  lead programmer Soren. Neither page establishes that Conquests retained every
  base-game detail. The distance discussion attributes to Soren a modifier
  formed by scaling the ratio of challenger-capital-to-city distance over
  defender-capital-to-city distance by 2,000 and capping the ratio to
  0.25–4. CivWiki specifies these distances as `max(x, y) + 0.5 * min(x, y)`,
  where `x` and `y` are distances along the two diagonal map axes; it also says
  the Forbidden Palace and Secret Police HQ count as capitals. Thus
  `D = clamp(challenger_distance / defender_distance, 0.25, 4)`, and the
  formula denominator is `2000 * D`. `MapWrap::civ3_culture_distance` now
  implements the cited diagonal-axis metric and chooses the closest horizontal
  wrap image; deterministic checks cover an interior diagonal and the map
  seam. The seam rule is a topology-based inference, not a controlled
  Conquests observation. BLDG[82] Secret Police HQ is now mapped to catalog
  entry 90, using its installed BIQ cost and Espionage requirement, so the
  candidate calculation can recognize all three reported capital buildings.
  When multiple capital buildings exist, choosing the closest is an explicit
  implementation inference; zero-distance handling remains unresolved. The
  CivFanatics Conquests FAQ says WLTKD requires
  size at least 6, no unhappy or resisting citizens, at least half the citizens
  happy, and food production of at least 2 per citizen. These criteria provide
  a reverse-engineered way to derive `H`, but the full formula and its
  interactions remain unconfirmed in the installed Conquests game. Sources:
  [CivWiki, “Flip probability”](https://civ-wiki.de/wiki/Flipwahrscheinlichkeit_(Civ3))
  and [CivFanatics Conquests FAQ](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/frequently-asked-questions-civ3-play-the-world-conquests.170282/).
  `city_meets_wltkd_criteria` encodes the Civilopedia's no-starvation,
  no-resister, no-unhappy, and more-happy-than-content conditions, with the
  commonly reported size-6 threshold from the FAQ. `candidate_city_flip_probability_to`
  now derives population, foreign-controlled radius tiles, city and civilization
  culture, disorder, WLTKD, garrison, and capital distances from simulation
  state before applying the candidate arithmetic. A deterministic scenario
  covers those derived inputs, the garrison reduction, and doubled chance when
  the challenger has greater local culture. This candidate calculation is
  still not called during turn resolution. It returns unresolved (`None`) when
  multiple foreign nationalities are present because the formula does not say
  whether non-challenger foreigners contribute to `F`. Zero-total-culture or
  invalid capital-distance ratios also return unresolved (`None`) because
  available sources do not establish the engine behavior for those undefined
  inputs. The candidate formula has
  not been controlled-observed in Conquests. Firaxis staff's 2002 explanation
  identifies land-based combat units with positive attack and defense as the
  garrison factor, and says each garrison subtracts from the chance. A 2004
  CivFanatics discussion tagged for Conquests reports that units in a flipped
  city are destroyed and that a new defender appears; it does not establish
  the new unit's selection algorithm. No official Conquests passage or
  controlled observation available here confirms those outcomes. Unit handling
  and exact turn-resolution timing therefore remain unresolved. Sources:
  [Dan Magaha (Firaxis), “Culture-Flipping Exposed”]
  (https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/culture-flipping-exposed.14337/),
  [CivFanatics, “Armies captured in culture flips?”]
  (https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/armies-captured-in-culture-flips.90904/).

## Required controlled observations

Before implementing formulas not explicit above, create isolated Conquests
scenarios and record the starting save, BIQ hash, turn/year, settings, action,
and observed result for:

1. Per-building culture output, obsolete building behavior, and mobilization
   half-output.
